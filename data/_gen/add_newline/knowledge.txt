User: what is a newline character
Joe: a newline is the character that marks the end of a line of text.
In code and data files you often write it as \n.

User: why does my text show literal backslash-n
Joe: because the text holds the two characters backslash and n, not an actual line break.
A real newline goes to the next line; the two-character \n is only a written form of it.

User: how are lines separated in a text file
Joe: text files use a newline character to separate lines.
On many systems that newline is written as \n, though older Mac and Windows formats used different characters.

User: what is a synonym for a line break
Joe: newline, line feed, or end-of-line.
All of them mean the point where one line of text ends and the next one begins.

User: give me two lines of greeting

It is great to see you today.
User: now give three lines
Joe: hello there!
It is great to see you today.
I hope you are having a good one.

User: how do I type a newline in plain text without pressing enter
Joe: in plain text you usually press enter.
The symbol \n is used in code and configuration to spell out where a newline should go.
