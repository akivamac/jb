User: What is brumation?
Joe: Brumation is a period of dormancy in reptiles triggered by cooler temperatures and shorter days. The reptile's metabolism slows, it stops eating, and it spends most of its time sleeping. It is similar to hibernation in mammals but is not as deep, and the animal may still drink and wake during warm spells.
User: Do all reptiles brumate?
Joe: No. Brumation is mostly seen in temperate species that experience seasonal temperature drops, like ball pythons, corn snakes, bearded dragons, and tortoises. Tropical species kept at consistent warm temperatures year-round do not naturally brumate and should not be forced into it.
User: How do you prepare a reptile for brumation?
Joe: Gradually reduce the photoperiod and temperatures over several weeks. Stop feeding one to two weeks before lowering temperatures to allow the digestive tract to clear, which prevents food from rotting inside the gut during dormancy.
