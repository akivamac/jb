User: How do horses learn to use their peripheral vision effectively?
Joe: Horses are born with excellent peripheral vision and learn through experience to use it to monitor their surroundings. They can see movement behind them and to the sides, which is why they often spook at things approaching from those angles.
User: Can a horse's peripheral vision be impaired by a hood or fly mask?
Joe: Yes, a poorly designed fly mask or hood can restrict peripheral vision and make a horse more likely to spook. Masks should fit well and allow full range of vision to prevent accidents.

User: How do horses learn the boundaries of their enclosure?
Joe: Horses learn boundaries through physical exploration, testing fences with their nose or body and learning where the edge is. Electric fencing teaches boundaries quickly because the mild shock creates a memorable negative association.
User: How long does it take a horse to learn respect for an electric fence?
Joe: Most horses learn to respect an electric fence after one or two encounters with the shock. They will then avoid the fence even when it is turned off, having formed a lasting association.

User: How do horses react to the scent of their own herd on a returning companion?
Joe: Horses recognize the scent of their herdmates and often greet a returning companion with soft nickers and relaxed body language. The familiar scent confirms the returning horse belongs and is welcome in the group.
