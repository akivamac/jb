User: What causes two high tides a day

User: Does the sun ever add to the effect

User: What is a spring tide
Joe: A spring tide is an extra-large tide that happens when the sun, moon, and Earth line up. The sun's pull adds to the moon's pull, making the highest high tides and lowest low tides.
User: When do the smaller neap tides happen
Joe: Neap tides happen when the sun and moon are at right angles to Earth. Their pulls partly cancel, producing much smaller tidal swings.

Joe: Near the equator sunlight hits the ground more directly, concentrating the heat. Near the poles the same light spreads over a wider, tilted area.
User: How does that affect weather
Joe: The hot equator drives rising air and heavy rainfall, creating tropical forests. The cooler poles sink cold air, which is why ice caps are so dry.

User: How long is the cycle from one new moon to the next
Joe: From one new moon to the next takes about 29.5 days, called a synodic month. This is longer than the moon's 27-day orbit because Earth also moves around the sun.
User: Why are months called months
Joe: The word month comes from "moon," since early calendars tracked the lunar cycle. The moon and month are the same word in many languages.

User: Why does Mars have two tiny moons
Joe: Phobos and Deimos may be captured asteroids from the nearby asteroid belt. Their lumpy, cratered shapes match typical space rocks.
User: What is happening to Phobos
Joe: Phobos is slowly spiraling toward Mars and will likely crash or break apart. That could give Mars a ring in tens of millions of years.

User: What is the difference between a star and a planet
Joe: A star shines from its own internal fusion, while a planet shines only by reflecting a star's light. That is one reason planets behave so differently in the sky.
User: How can you tell them apart at night
Joe: Stars twinkle while planets usually glow with a steady light. Planets also drift slowly among the stars over weeks.

User: Why do planets not twinkle
Joe: Planets are close enough to appear as tiny disks rather than point-like sparks. The atmosphere muddles the pinpoints of stars but not those slightly larger disks.
User: What about a planet near the horizon
Joe: Even then a planet stays steadier than a nearby star. The extra air near the horizon disturbs all light from space more.

User: Where does the word ocean come from
Joe: Ocean comes from "Oceanus," the Greek god of the great world river believed to circle the Earth. The name carried over to the seas we call oceans.

User: Where does the word dollar come from
Joe: Dollar comes from the "thaler," a silver coin first minted in a town in what is now the Czech Republic. The name was shortened and adopted worldwide.

Joe: The Spanish dollar, widely used before US money, likely inspired the sign. Some trace it to the peso's "S" with a slash.

User: Where does the word robot come from
Joe: Robot comes from the Czech word "robota," meaning forced labor. The playwright Karel Capek coined it in 1920 for his play about artificial workers.

Joe: He used it in the play but credited his brother Josef with suggesting the term. The play spread the word into every language.

User: Where does the word alphabet come from

Joe: It was one of the first to write every sound with a letter, including vowels. That made reading and writing much easier to learn.

User: What is the farthest spacecraft from Earth
Joe: Voyager 1, launched in 1977, is the farthest human-made object, sailing beyond the solar system. Its twin Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to visit all four giant planets.

User: How hot is the sun's core
Joe: The sun's core reaches about 27 million degrees Fahrenheit. That extreme heat and pressure drive the fusion that powers the sun.

User: What are sunspots
Joe: Sunspots are cooler, darker patches on the sun's surface caused by strong magnetic fields. They follow an 11-year cycle of activity.

User: Why is Venus the brightest planet
Joe: Venus is bright because it is close to us and wrapped in highly reflective clouds. Its thick cloud cover bounces much of the sunlight back at us.

User: When can you see Mercury
Joe: Mercury is visible only near dawn or dusk because it stays close to the sun. It is best seen just after sunset or just before sunrise.

User: What is the Seven Sisters star cluster
Joe: The Pleiades, called the Seven Sisters, is a star cluster in the constellation Taurus. It holds hundreds of young blue stars shining within a dust cloud.

User: What are Uranus's rings like
Joe: Uranus has a faint set of narrow rings discovered in 1977. They are much darker and thinner than Saturn's.

User: Which planet is less dense than water
Joe: Saturn is less dense than water, so it would float in a giant bathtub. It is the only planet in the solar system with a density below water's.

User: Why is Pluto not a planet anymore
Joe: Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006 because it has not cleared its orbit of other objects. It shares its neighborhood with other icy bodies.

Joe: The United States completed the Panama Canal in 1914 after an earlier French attempt failed. It lets ships skip the long trip around South America.

User: Who designed the Golden Gate Bridge
Joe: The Golden Gate Bridge was designed by engineer Joseph Strauss, with chief engineer Charles Ellis. It opened in 1937 over the entrance to San Francisco Bay.

Joe: The Sydney Opera House was designed by the Danish architect Jorn Utzon. Its sail-like shells opened in 1973.

User: Who built the Burj Khalifa
Joe: The Burj Khalifa, the world's tallest building, was built by a Dubai-led project and opened in 2010. It stands over 2,700 feet tall.

User: Who carved Mount Rushmore
Joe: Mount Rushmore was carved by sculptor Gutzon Borglum and his team between 1927 and 1941. It shows the faces of four US presidents.

Joe: Sweden has the most islands, estimated at over 200,000. Most are small and uninhabited.

Joe: Gymnasium comes from the Greek word "gymnos," meaning naked. Greek athletes trained without clothes in their gymnasiums.

Joe: Sincere may come from the Latin "sine cera," meaning without wax. Sculptors may have used wax to hide flaws, so without it meant genuine.

User: Where does the word cash come from
Joe: Cash likely comes from the Italian "cassa," meaning a strongbox or container for money. The word later shifted to mean the money itself.

User: Why do cats see well in the dark
Joe: Cats' eyes have more light-catching rods and a reflective layer behind the retina. That mirror, the tapetum, gives their eyes a greenish night glow.

User: Why do dogs tilt their heads
Joe: Dogs may tilt their heads to hear better or to see around the front of their muzzle. It helps them read faces and locate where sounds come from.

User: How do bees find their way home
Joe: Bees navigate using the sun's position, landmarks, and patterns of polarized light. They also perform waggle dances to tell hive-mates where flowers are.

User: Why do butterflies have bright blue wings
Joe: Butterfly wings get blues and greens from microscopic structure instead of pigment. Tiny scales bend and scatter light to create that color.

User: Why do some frogs glow
Joe: Many frogs glow under ultraviolet light, a trait called biofluorescence. The glow may help them recognize mates and signal each other.

User: How do penguins swim so fast
Joe: Penguins fly through the water by flapping their flippers like wings. Their streamlined bodies and strong muscles let them outswim most fish.
