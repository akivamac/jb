User: How do you care for a leopard gecko?
Joe: Leopard geckos need a 20-gallon tank with a warm side (88-92 degrees Fahrenheit) and a cool side (75-80 degrees). They require a hide on each side, a shallow water dish, and calcium with vitamin D3 dusted on their insects. Feed them crickets, mealworms, or dubia roaches every other day.

User: What do leopard geckos eat?
Joe: Leopard geckos eat live insects primarily crickets, mealworms, dubia roaches, and silkworms. Occasional waxworms make a treat. All feeder insects should be dusted with calcium powder at most feedings and with a multivitamin once a week. Adult geckos eat every 2-3 days.

User: Why do leopard geckos drop their tails?
Joe: Leopard geckos drop their tails as a defense mechanism called autotomy. When grabbed by a predator, the tail detaches and continues wriggling, distracting the predator while the gecko escapes. The tail regenerates over several weeks as a cartilage-supported replacement.

User: Do leopard gecko tails grow back?
Joe: Yes, leopard gecko tails do regenerate, but the regrown tail looks different. It is usually shorter, smoother, and bulb-shaped rather than tapered. The regenerated tail contains cartilage instead of bone and may be a slightly different color than the original.

User: How long do leopard geckos live?
Joe: Leopard geckos typically live 10 to 20 years in captivity with proper care. Some have lived over 30 years. Females may live slightly shorter lives due to the energy demands of egg production. Proper temperature, diet, and husbandry significantly affect lifespan.

User: What is a reptile?
Joe: A reptile is a cold-blooded vertebrate animal with scales or scutes that typically lays eggs and breathes air. Reptiles include snakes, lizards, turtles, crocodilians, and tuatara. They regulate body temperature through behavior like basking in the sun.

User: How do reptiles regulate body temperature?
Joe: Reptiles are ectothermic, meaning they rely on external heat sources to regulate body temperature. They bask in sunlight to warm up, move to shade or water to cool down, and some change color to absorb or reflect heat. This is behavioral thermoregulation.

User: What is the difference between a snake and a lizard?
Joe: Snakes are legless lizards with elongated bodies, no eyelids, and no external ear openings. Lizards typically have legs, movable eyelids, and ear openings. Some lizards like glass lizards are also legless, but snakes are distinguished by their highly flexible jaws and forked tongues.

User: Can snakes hear?
Joe: Yes, snakes can hear, but they detect only low-frequency vibrations from 80 to 1,000 Hertz. They lack external ears but have internal ear structures connected to their jawbone, which picks up ground vibrations. They respond more to vibrations than airborne sound.

User: How do snakes smell?
Joe: Snakes smell using their forked tongue. They flick their tongue to collect scent particles from the air and ground, then press the tongue tips into the Jacobson's organ on the roof of the mouth, which analyzes the chemical information and tells the snake which direction the scent comes from.

User: What is a constriction?
Joe: Constriction is how non-venomous snakes like boas and pythons kill their prey. They coil their body around the prey and tighten each time the prey exhales, eventually preventing the lungs from expanding. This causes rapid cardiac arrest rather than suffocation.

User: Are all snakes venomous?
Joe: No, the majority of snake species are non-venomous. Only about 600 of the over 3,000 snake species are venomous, and of those, only about 200 can seriously harm humans. Non-venomous snakes kill prey by constriction, swallowing alive, or simply overpowering small animals.

User: How do venomous snakes deliver venom?
Joe: Venomous snakes deliver venom through hollow or grooved fangs. Vipers have long folding front fangs that fold back when the mouth is closed. Elapids like cobras and coral snakes have shorter fixed front fangs. Some rear-fanged snakes deliver venom through grooved back teeth.

User: What is the difference between venom and poison?
Joe: Venom is injected into another animal through fangs, stingers, or spines. Poison is absorbed or ingested and is dangerous when touched or eaten. A snake is venomous if it bites you and you get sick. A snake is poisonous if you bite it and you get sick.

User: What do turtles eat?
Joe: Diet depends on the species. Aquatic turtles like red-eared sliders eat a mix of commercial pellets, leafy greens, insects, and occasional fish. Land tortoises are mostly herbivorous, eating grasses, weeds, and vegetables. Sea turtles eat jellyfish, sponges, or seagrass depending on the species.

User: How long do turtles live?
Joe: Turtles are among the longest-lived animals. Many species live 50 to 100 years or more. Giant tortoises can exceed 150 years. Box turtles commonly reach 50 to 100 years. Aquatic turtles like red-eared sliders typically live 20 to 40 years in captivity.

User: Do turtles retract into their shells?
Joe: Most turtles can pull their head and legs partially or fully into their shell for protection. Box turtles have a hinge on their plastron (bottom shell) that allows them to close completely. Sea turtles cannot retract because their shells are streamlined for swimming.

User: How do crocodiles differ from alligators?
Joe: Crocodiles have narrow V-shaped snouts, visible teeth when the mouth is closed, and prefer saltwater. Alligators have wide U-shaped snouts, visible upper teeth only when the mouth is closed, and prefer freshwater. Crocodiles are generally more aggressive.

User: How fast can a crocodile run?
Joe: Crocodiles can run surprisingly fast on land for short distances. Most species reach 10 to 14 miles per hour in short bursts. The Australian freshwater crocodile can gallop at up to 17 miles per hour. They tire quickly and cannot maintain speed.

User: What is a tuatara?
Joe: The tuatara is a reptile native to New Zealand that is the sole survivor of an ancient order distinct from lizards and snakes. It looks lizard-like but has unique features including a third parietal eye on top of its head and teeth fused to the jaw bone. It can live over 100 years.

User: What is brumation?
Joe: Brumation is the reptile equivalent of hibernation. During cold months, reptiles enter a period of reduced activity and metabolism. They hide in burrows or rock crevices, eat little or nothing, and survive on stored fat. Unlike true hibernation, they may wake on warm days.

User: How do iguanas regulate temperature?
Joe: Green iguanas are ectothermic and rely on basking to raise their body temperature to 85-95 degrees Fahrenheit. They spend hours on branches in direct sunlight. When overheated, they seek shade or water. At night, their body temperature drops to match ambient temperature.

User: What do iguanas eat?
Joe: Green iguanas are primarily herbivorous. They eat leafy greens like collard greens, mustard greens, and dandelion greens, as well as squash, berries, and flowers. Young iguanas eat more insects for protein. They should avoid spinach, rhubarb, and high-oxalate greens.

User: How big do iguanas get?
Joe: Green iguanas can reach 5 to 7 feet long from nose to tail tip, with males growing larger than females. They typically weigh 10 to 20 pounds. Some exceptional males reach 6 to 8 feet. Their tail makes up about two-thirds of their total length.

User: What is the difference between a gecko and a lizard?
Joe: All geckos are lizards, but not all lizards are geckos. Geckos are a specific group of lizards that typically have no eyelids, adhesive toe pads for climbing, vocal abilities, and the ability to lay eggs in hard shells or give live birth depending on species.

User: Can geckos climb glass?
Joe: Yes, many geckos can climb smooth vertical surfaces including glass. They use millions of tiny hair-like structures called setae on their toe pads. Each seta splits into hundreds of spatula-shaped tips that create van der Waals forces with the surface, allowing them to stick.

User: What is a bearded dragon?
Joe: A bearded dragon is a popular pet lizard native to Australia. They are omnivorous, eating insects and vegetables. They get their name from the spiny scales under their throat that they puff out when threatened. They typically live 10 to 15 years in captivity.

User: How do you care for a bearded dragon?
Joe: Bearded dragons need a 40-gallon tank minimum for adults with a basking spot of 100-110 degrees Fahrenheit and a cool side of 80-85. They need UVB lighting for 12 to 14 hours daily. Feed them crickets, dubia roaches, and dark leafy greens. Provide a shallow water dish.

User: What is the largest living reptile?
Joe: The saltwater crocodile is the largest living reptile. Males can reach 20 feet in length and weigh over 2,000 pounds. Some exceptional individuals have been reported at 23 feet and nearly 3,000 pounds. They are found in coastal waters of Southeast Asia and Australia.

User: Do reptiles lay eggs or give live birth?
Joe: Most reptiles lay eggs (oviparous), but some give live birth (viviparous or ovoviviparous). Boas, garter snakes, and some skinks give live birth. The method depends on the species. Even egg-laying species vary in whether they bury eggs or guard them.

User: What is egg binding in reptiles?
Joe: Egg binding (dystocia) is a condition where a female reptile cannot lay her eggs. It can be caused by calcium deficiency, dehydration, stress, infertile eggs, or infection. It is a veterinary emergency as the retained eggs can become infected or compress organs.

User: What do snake droppings look like?
Joe: Snake droppings consist of a dark brown or black fecal portion and a white urate portion. The white part is uric acid, which is how reptiles excrete nitrogenous waste instead of liquid urine. Snake poop may contain bones or fur from prey.

User: How do reptiles shed their skin?
Joe: Reptiles shed their skin periodically as they grow. Snakes shed in one piece, while lizards shed in patches. The old skin loosens as a new layer forms underneath. Proper humidity helps complete sheds. Retained shed around eyes or toes can cause circulation problems.

User: What is the difference between a turtle and a tortoise?
Joe: Turtles are aquatic or semi-aquatic with webbed feet or flippers and streamlined shells. Tortoises are fully land-dwelling with stumpy legs and dome-shaped shells. Turtles eat more protein (insects, fish) while tortoises are primarily herbivorous.

User: Can turtles drown?
Joe: Yes, turtles can drown. Sea turtles and aquatic turtles breathe air and must surface regularly. If a turtle cannot reach the surface due to deep water, injury, or being trapped, it can drown. Pet aquatic turtles need shallow areas where they can rest with their head above water.

User: How fast can a box turtle retract into its shell?
Joe: Box turtles can fully close their shell in about a minute when alarmed. The hinged plastron folds up tight against the carapace, sealing the turtle inside like a box. Younger turtles retract faster than older ones. They may stay closed for hours if they feel threatened.
