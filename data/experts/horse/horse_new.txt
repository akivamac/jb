User: How much should I feed my horse?
Joe: An average horse eats about 1.5 to 2.5 percent of its body weight in feed daily. A 1,000 pound horse needs 15 to 25 pounds of food. Most of this should be forage (hay or pasture). Add grain only if the horse needs more energy for work.

User: How often should I ride my horse?
Joe: This depends on the horse's fitness level and your goals. A fit horse can be ridden 5 to 6 days a week. A horse in light work is fine 3 to 4 days. Always include rest days. Overwork leads to physical and mental burnout for both horse and rider.

User: What should I look for when buying a horse?
Joe: Check the horse's temperament first, then conformation. Watch the horse move at walk, trot, and canter. Have a vet do a pre-purchase exam. Ask about the horse's history, training, and any vices. Consider your own skill level honestly.

User: How do I calm a nervous horse?
Joe: Speak softly and move slowly. Give the horse time to look at and process what frightens it. Do not punish fear. Use rhythmic, predictable movements. Exercise can burn off nervous energy. Consistent, patient handling builds trust over time.

User: What is the best bedding for horses?
Joe: Straw is traditional, cheap, and comfortable but needs frequent changing. Shavings (pine or cedar) are absorbent and clean but avoid cedar shavings for horses with respiratory issues. Wood pellets are economical when soaked. Choose based on your barn setup and budget.

User: How do I groom my horse?
Joe: Start with a curry comben circular motions to loosen dirt. Use a hard brush to flick away debris, then a soft brush for finishing. Clean hooves with a hoof pick daily. Brush the mane and tail with a detangler. Grooming is bonding time, not just cleaning.

User: What are signs of colic in horses?
Joe: Colic signs include pawing, looking at the flank, rolling, refusing food, a distended belly, sweating, and restlessness. Mild colic may resolve with walking. Severe or persistent colic is a veterinary emergency. Colic is the leading cause of death in horses.

User: How do I prevent colic?
Joe: Prevent colic by providing constant access to clean water, feeding consistent meals at regular times, making diet changes slowly, providing regular exercise, and checking for foreign objects in hay. Stress and dehydration are major colic triggers.

User: What is the difference between horse gaits?
Joe: Walk is four-beat and the slowest gait. Trot is two-beat and bouncy. Canter is three-beat and smooth. Gallop is four-beat and the fastest. Some breeds have special gaits like the Missouri Fox Trotter's fox trot or the Icelandic horse's tölt.

User: How long is a horse pregnant?
Joe: A mare's gestation period is approximately 320 to 370 days, averaging about 340 days or roughly 11 months. Foaling usually occurs in spring. Watch for signs of foaling including udder development, relaxation of the vulva, and wax-like droplets on teats.

User: What is the best tack for a beginner rider?
Joe: A well-fitting all-purpose saddle and a simple snaffle bit are best for beginners. The saddle should support the rider securely without being restrictive. A snaffle bit provides clear, gentle communication. Safety stirrups and a properly fitted helmet are essential.

User: How do I fit a saddle?
Joe: The saddle should clear the withers with at least two to three fingers of space. It should distribute weight evenly across the back without creating pressure points. It should not extend past the last rib. Watch for signs of poor fit including soreness, white marks, or behavioral issues under saddle.

User: What is horse herd behavior?
Joe: Horses are herd animals with a social hierarchy. A lead mare usually directs the group's movement while a stallion protects. Horses establish rank through body language, not fighting. Removing a horse from its herd causes stress. They feel safer and calmer with companions.

User: How do horses communicate?
Joe: Horses communicate through body language, ear position, tail movement, vocalization, and facial expressions. A pinned ear signals aggression. A nicker is a friendly greeting. A wide eye shows fear. Understanding these signals helps you read your horse's mood and intentions.

User: What is the best age to start riding a horse?
Joe: Most horses are ready to begin light training around 2 to 3 years old. However, a horse can be started under saddle at 4 to 5 when its bones are more developed. Starting too young can cause long-term soundness issues. Some breeds mature later than others.

User: How do I treat a hoof abscess?
Joe: Soak the hoof in warm water with Epsom salt for 20 minutes twice daily. Apply a poultice and wrap the hoof. The abscess usually drains on its own within a few days. If it does not improve or the horse is very lame, call the farrier or vet.

User: What is laminitis?
Joe: Laminitis is inflammation of the laminae, the tissue connecting the hoof wall to the coffin bone. It causes severe lameness and can be life-threatening. Causes include overeating grain, obesity, and metabolic conditions. Foundering (rotation of the coffin bone) is the severe form.

User: How do I prevent laminitis?
Joe: Prevent laminitis by limiting access to lush pasture, avoiding grain overload, maintaining a healthy weight, providing regular exercise, and working with your vet to manage metabolic conditions like Cushing's disease and insulin resistance.

User: What is a farrier?
Joe: A farrier is a specialist in horse hoof care and shoeing. They trim hooves every 6 to 8 weeks and fit shoes when needed. A good farrier is essential for soundness. Find one experienced with your horse's breed and discipline.

User: How do I know if my horse is in pain?
Joe: Signs of pain in horses include changes in behavior like reluctance to move, pinned ears, grinding teeth, flinching, changes in appetite, and altered gait. Horses hide pain instinctively, so subtle changes matter. A vet can help diagnose the source.

User: What is the best way to lead a horse?
Joe: Walk at the horse's shoulder, not in front of it. Hold the lead rope close to the halter with one hand and the excess in the other. Do not wrap the rope around your hand. Walk confidently and the horse should follow. Use body language and voice commands together.
